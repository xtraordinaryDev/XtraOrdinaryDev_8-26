How Much Does a Website Cost in Minneapolis?

By Xavier Thurman · September 8, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer: in the Twin Cities in 2026, a small-business website runs anywhere from a few hundred dollars a year on a do-it-yourself builder to well into five figures for a custom-designed, custom-coded site with integrations. The spread is that wide because "a website" can mean very different things. This guide explains what sits in each price band, what actually moves the number, and how to get a quote that will not grow after you sign.

The four price bands you will see in Minneapolis

Every quote you get from a Minneapolis designer, freelancer, or agency falls into one of these buckets. The numbers below are typical market ranges we see when clients bring us competing quotes – not our own price list.

1. DIY builders (Wix, Squarespace, GoDaddy): roughly $200–$600 per year

You pick a template, drop in your text and photos, and pay a monthly subscription forever. It is the cheapest way to have something online, and for a brand-new side business that can be the right call. The trade-offs: everyone in your category has the same three templates, the sites are slow on phones, you cannot fully control the SEO, and if you ever leave the platform, you leave with nothing – the site is not portable.

2. Freelancer with a WordPress theme: roughly $1,500–$5,000

A freelancer installs WordPress, buys a premium theme, adds a page-builder plugin, and customizes the colors and content. You get more flexibility than a DIY builder and a site you technically own. The risks are the ones we hear about most often: the freelancer disappears, the plugins stop being updated, the site gets hacked or slows to a crawl, and the next developer wants to start over because nobody can untangle the theme.

3. Custom-designed, hand-coded site from a studio: roughly $5,000–$20,000

Design that starts from your brand and your customer instead of a template, clean code without a page builder, real mobile-first layout, technical SEO built in, and conversion elements – click-to-call, estimate forms, reviews – placed on purpose. This is the band where most established Minneapolis businesses land: contractors, clinics, law firms, restaurants, nonprofits, B2B services. It costs more up front and almost always costs less over five years, because there is no subscription, no plugin maintenance, and no rebuild in year three.

4. E-commerce, web applications, and large marketing sites: $20,000 and up

Online stores with inventory and payments, event platforms with ticketing, customer portals, booking systems, or sites with dozens of pages and multiple languages. These are software projects that happen to live at a web address, and they are scoped like software: by feature, not by page count.

What actually drives the price

  • Custom design vs. a template. The single biggest factor. Custom means someone designs your layout, type, and imagery for your business; a template means you are adapting someone else's.
  • Number of pages. A five-page site (home, services, about, work, contact) is the sweet spot for most local businesses. Each additional service or location page adds design and writing time – but also adds a page that can rank on Google.
  • Content and photography. Writing that sells and photos that are not stock are often the part clients underestimate. Some studios include copywriting; many quote it separately. Ask.
  • Integrations. Online booking, payment processing, CRM connections, review feeds, quote calculators, and chat each add scope. None is expensive alone; five of them together is a different project.
  • SEO work. "SEO-friendly" should be free – clean code, fast load, proper titles, a sitemap. Keyword research, location pages, and ongoing content are real work and are priced as such.
  • Who does it. A large downtown agency carries account managers and overhead; a small studio carries less. Neither is automatically better. What matters is who will actually build your site and whether you can talk to that person.

The hidden costs most quotes leave out

When you compare a $3,000 quote with a $9,000 quote, make sure you are comparing the same five years, not the same launch day.

  • Subscriptions. Page-builder licenses, premium plugins, form tools, and "pro" features add up to hundreds per year on a typical WordPress site – forever.
  • Maintenance retainers. Many agencies require a monthly maintenance fee to keep WordPress updated and secure. A hand-coded site has nothing to update.
  • Hosting. Reasonable, fast hosting for a small site should cost very little. Be suspicious of quotes that bundle hosting at a premium.
  • The rebuild. Template sites tend to be rebuilt every 3–4 years because they get slow, dated, or unmaintainable. Custom-coded sites tend to get refreshed, not replaced.
  • Ownership. Read the contract: do you own the design files and the code? Can you move to another host or developer? If the answer is no, the price is higher than it looks.

How to get a Minneapolis website quote you can trust

  1. Start with the goal, not the pages. "More estimate requests from homeowners in the west metro" is a brief a good studio can price. "A website" is not.
  2. Ask for a fixed price with a written scope. Hourly estimates on creative work almost always grow. A fixed price with a page list and a list of integrations protects you.
  3. Ask who is building it. Meet the actual designer or developer before you sign.
  4. Ask to see live sites, not mockups. Open them on your phone. Are they fast? Do they look like they belong to different businesses, or the same template?
  5. Ask about ownership and what happens after launch. You should own everything and be able to edit your own content.

The bottom line

For an established Minneapolis business that depends on its website to generate calls and leads, the middle band – a custom-designed, hand-coded site – is usually the best value over any horizon longer than a year. It costs more than a template on day one and less than a template by year three, and it is the only option that is actually built around your customers.
