WordPress runs a large share of the internet, and it is not going anywhere. It is also behind most of the slow, hacked, or abandoned websites that Minneapolis businesses bring to us for a rescue. Both of those things are true at once. This is an honest comparison from a studio that builds custom sites for a living but has no interest in selling you the wrong thing.

What "WordPress" usually means for a small business

In practice, a small-business WordPress site is rarely just WordPress. It is WordPress plus a purchased theme, plus a page-builder plugin (Elementor, Divi, WPBakery), plus 15–30 other plugins for forms, SEO, caching, security, sliders, and galleries. Each of those is a separate piece of software with its own updates, its own bugs, and its own security history. That stack is what you are really evaluating.

What "custom-coded" means

A designer creates your layout for your brand, and a developer writes the HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for it directly – no theme, no page builder. The site is a set of clean files that any competent developer can read, and it can be hosted anywhere for next to nothing. If you need to edit content yourself, a lightweight content system can be added on top; it is not all-or-nothing.

Head to head

Speed

Custom wins, and it is not close. A hand-coded page ships only the code it needs. A page-builder page ships the builder's entire framework, the theme's CSS, and every plugin's scripts on every page. Speed is a Google ranking factor and a conversion factor: slow sites lose visitors on mobile before the page appears.

Security

Custom wins. There is no admin login to brute-force, no database to inject into, and no plugin with a disclosed vulnerability. WordPress can be run securely, but it requires someone to apply updates promptly forever – which is exactly the job that gets neglected once the site is "done."

Editing your own content

WordPress wins by default. Its editor is familiar and your staff can update a page without a developer. A custom site needs either a developer for changes or a content system added on top (a headless CMS or simple admin panel), which is a normal part of a custom build when the client needs it – but it must be planned.

SEO

Slight edge to custom, mostly because of speed and clean markup. WordPress with a good SEO plugin is perfectly capable of ranking; the problem is that the same plugin stack that makes it easy to manage also makes it slow. Structured data, proper headings, sitemaps, and metadata can be done well on either platform – they just have to actually be done.

Cost on day one

WordPress usually wins. A theme-based site from a freelancer can be live for a fraction of a custom build's price.

Cost over five years

Custom usually wins. Add up the premium theme and plugin renewals, the maintenance retainer, the hosting tier WordPress needs to be fast, the occasional cleanup after a hack, and the rebuild when the theme is abandoned. A custom site has none of those line items. Most of our redesign clients are replacing a WordPress site that cost less to build and more to own.

Ownership and portability

Custom wins. You own plain files you can host anywhere. WordPress sites are portable in theory; in practice, a page-builder site that leaves its builder falls apart, and many "we own your site" contracts from agencies mean the opposite.

When WordPress is the right choice

  • You publish a lot of content – dozens of posts a month – and several people need to edit it.
  • Your budget only stretches to a template today and the site is not yet critical to revenue.
  • You have someone in-house who will genuinely keep it updated and secured.
  • You need a specific WordPress-only ecosystem – a membership plugin, a particular e-commerce workflow, an LMS.

When a custom-coded site is the right choice

  • The website is how customers find and judge your business – contractors, clinics, professional services, restaurants, event and nonprofit organizations.
  • You want the fastest possible site on mobile and the search visibility that comes with it.
  • You are tired of plugin updates, security scares, and retainers.
  • You want a design that is unmistakably yours, not a theme everyone in your category also bought.
  • You plan to grow the site into something more – a portal, a booking system, an application.

The middle path most people do not know about

You do not have to choose between "fast but hard to edit" and "easy to edit but slow." A custom-coded front end with a lightweight content system behind the pages you change often – services, team, blog – gives you the speed and security of custom code with an editor for the content that actually changes. That is how we build most sites that need regular updates.

A quick decision checklist

  1. Does this site need to generate leads or sales? → Lean custom.
  2. Will you publish more than a few posts a month? → Plan a content system either way.
  3. Is there someone who will maintain WordPress every month, forever? If not → Lean custom.
  4. Is the budget only enough for a template right now? → WordPress or a builder is fine; plan the upgrade.
  5. Do you want to own something that works in five years without a rebuild? → Custom.
