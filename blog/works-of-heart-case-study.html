Case Study: Works of Heart

By Xavier Thurman · September 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Works of Heart is a gala and fine-art auction benefiting Saint John's Abbey in Collegeville, Minnesota, celebrating Father Jerome Tupa – a Benedictine monk and painter whose bold, vivid canvases fill the event's collection. The evening takes place October 22, 2026 at The Hutton House on Medicine Lake, with a live performance by Amy Grant. Our job was to build the website that would introduce the event, present the art, and sell the room.

The brief

The event committee needed one site to do four different jobs for four different audiences:

  • Invite – give recipients of the printed invitation a digital version worth forwarding, and make buying a ticket effortless.
  • Present the art – show the auction lots and the wider collection with the respect a gallery would, on a phone as well as a desktop.
  • Tell the story – introduce Father Tupa, his fifty years of painting, his connection to The Saint John's Bible, and the Abbey's mission that every dollar supports.
  • Inform – venue, schedule, menu, the cause, and sponsors, without burying the call to action.

It also had a hard deadline – invitations were going out – and a visual standard set by the paintings themselves. A template was never an option.

Design: let the paintings set the rules

We started from the canvases. Father Tupa's work is saturated, layered, and full of movement, so the site uses full-bleed crops of the paintings as its backgrounds and takes its palette directly from them – the teal, cobalt, and red that recur across the collection become the bands that carry the date, the title, and the countdown. Typography is editorial: a high-contrast serif for headlines against small, tracked uppercase labels, the same language as a fine-art catalogue or an invitation card.

The result reads like a printed program that happens to be interactive. Every section opens on a painting; every piece of information sits on a clean panel over it.

Features we built

The invitation, as an object

Rather than pasting the invitation text into a page, we built the invitation itself: an envelope that opens on the page to reveal the card and the booklet, with the host's personal invitation and the evening's program. It is the first thing a visitor meets after the hero, and it is the emotional center of the site.

Live countdown and event details

A countdown to the evening, the date and venue, cocktail hour, dinner, auction, and entertainment presented as a save-the-date panel with the ticket link and directions – the information a guest needs, exactly where they expect it.

Featured lots and the auction catalogue

A "Featured Lots" gallery presents the headline paintings with title, medium, and size, and links through to the full catalogue. A second collection, "Beyond the Paintings," presents the other lots – illuminated pages from The Saint John's Bible, wood-fired stoneware, and an original Picasso print – with the same care.

The artist, the Bible, the cause

Dedicated sections introduce Father Tupa in his own words ("We are all on a pilgrimage. That is the human condition."), the Abbey's role in The Saint John's Bible, and the mission every lot supports – with the sponsors whose partnership makes the evening possible.

Live entertainment

A section announcing Amy Grant's performance, written to convey what an extraordinary evening this is without competing with the art for attention.

Engineering: a gallery that loads on a phone

The design depends on large, high-quality images of paintings – the one thing that makes a website slow. We hand-coded the site rather than using a page builder, sized and compressed every image for the screen it appears on, loaded artwork lazily as the visitor scrolls, and kept the code lean enough that the page is responsive on a phone on cellular data. The layout is mobile-first: the envelope, the catalogue, and the countdown all work with a thumb.

Under the hood the site ships with the technical SEO we build into every project – descriptive titles and metadata, structured data for the event, clean semantic markup, and a sitemap – so that searches for the artist, the event, and the Abbey's gala find it.

What it delivered

A site the committee could send with the invitation and be proud of, a catalogue the art deserved, and a single clear path to a ticket from every section. We will update this case study with results after the evening.
