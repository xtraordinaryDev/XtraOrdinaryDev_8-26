Custom Software Development in Minneapolis, MN

The development team for Minneapolis companies that don’t have one

Most companies in the Twin Cities with 200 to 2,000 people are in the same spot. A managed IT provider runs the network and Microsoft 365. An ERP runs the books. Everything in between runs on spreadsheets, email and three people who know how. There is an IT Director, a budget, and a list of things done by hand. What there isn’t is a developer.

X-traordinary Development is that developer. We build custom software and AI for mid-sized companies in engineering and construction, agriculture and co-ops, manufacturing, distribution and professional services, inside the Microsoft and Azure environment they already pay for. Everything we build is theirs; we run it for as long as they want it run.

We are currently the entire development function for a 1,000-person Minnesota engineering firm, and we built internal applications at CHS Inc. and U.S. Bank before that. Based in Minneapolis, we sit down with your team in the metro and run the engagement from here.

What we build

Five things, in the words your team would use

Always inside your tenant, always with a person approving. We start with the process that costs you the most hours.

An assistant over your own documents

Standards, contracts, past projects, the handbook: ask a question, get an answer from your files with a citation. Runs over SharePoint, file shares and your systems of record.

Research and intelligence feeds

Funding programs, bids, regulations, suppliers, competitors: whatever your team reads by hand today, watched automatically and summarised every morning.

Document generation from your data

First drafts of proposals, reports and client memos from your past work, in your format. A person edits before anything goes out.

Workflow automation with a person in the loop

The spreadsheet process three people run becomes a system that routes, reminds and records. Intake, approvals, scheduling, invoicing, onboarding, compliance.

Integrations and dashboards

Deltek, Epicor, NetSuite, Dynamics, Microsoft 365 and the new tools actually talking to each other, with a dashboard your leadership reads on Monday.

Internal applications

Portals, field apps, back-office tools and the custom systems that off-the-shelf software never quite fits. The work we did for years inside U.S. Bank and CHS.

Questions we get on the first call

Straight answers

Our managed IT provider handles this. Why would we need you?

They run your infrastructure, and they are good at it. Ask them who builds the custom tools their clients ask for. It is usually us, or nobody. Most of our introductions come from managed IT providers for exactly that reason.

Why not just hire a developer?

That is $160K to $200K loaded, three to six months to find, and one generalist starting from zero. The retained function costs less, starts on a date, comes with response times in writing, and you can end it with notice. If you hire later, we help them take it over.

Is our data safe with AI in the picture?

Everything runs in your Azure tenant under your policies. Your data never leaves it and never trains anyone’s model. We put that in the contract.

Can we start smaller than the assessment?

The assessment is the small start. Two weeks, a ranked list of what is worth building, one tool shipped. It also tells you what not to build, which is usually worth more than the first tool.

Can you work hourly?

We can, at $175 an hour, without the roadmap or the response times. Most companies find the flat monthly number easier to budget and easier to defend to the board.

Which Minneapolis companies are a fit?

Companies of 200 to 2,000 people in engineering and construction, agriculture and co-ops, manufacturing, distribution and professional services, with an IT leader, a budget, and nobody who writes software. If you already have developers, or you need a website, we are not the right call.

Have a list of things done by hand?

Thirty minutes on the phone first. If it’s worth building, you’ll have the two-page assessment scope the next day.
