The five things we build

What “AI-native” means for a company with no developers

Not a chatbot bolted on. The process itself, rebuilt, with your data staying inside your tenant and a person approving every output that matters.

An assistant over your own documents

Standards, contracts, past projects, the employee handbook: ask a question, get an answer from your files with a citation. Runs over SharePoint, file shares and your systems of record.

Research and intelligence feeds

The sources your team reads by hand, watched automatically: funding programs, bids and RFPs, regulations, suppliers, competitors. Relevant items, every morning, in one place.

Document generation from your data

First drafts of proposals, reports, client memos and compliance documents, generated from your past work in your templates. A human edits before anything goes out.

Workflow automation with a person in the loop

Intake, approvals, scheduling, invoicing, onboarding, compliance: the spreadsheet process three people run becomes a system that routes, reminds and records. Your people approve; the system does the typing.

Integrations and dashboards

Deltek, Epicor, NetSuite, Dynamics, Microsoft 365 and the new tools, actually talking to each other, with a dashboard your leadership reads on Monday morning.

Internal applications

Portals, field apps, back-office tools and the custom system that doesn’t exist off the shelf. The work we did for years inside U.S. Bank and CHS Inc., now for companies that can’t hire for it.

The stack

Built on what you already pay for

Microsoft 365 and Azure first, because that’s where our clients live: Azure OpenAI and Azure AI Search, Entra ID for sign-in, SharePoint and Dataverse, SQL Server and PostgreSQL, .NET and C#, React and Next.js, Python for the data work. Where you run AWS or Google Cloud, the same patterns apply. Your data never leaves your tenant and never trains anyone’s model, and we put that in writing.

Who this is for

A fit, and not a fit

  • Engineering and construction, agriculture and co-ops, manufacturing, distribution and logistics, professional services, regional healthcare administration
  • An IT Director or CIO who reports to the CFO or COO, and a managed IT provider for infrastructure

Questions we get on the first call

Straight answers

“Our IT provider handles this.”

They run your infrastructure, and they’re good at it. Ask them who builds the custom tools their clients ask for. It’s usually us or nobody, and we work alongside them, not around them.

“We’ll just hire a developer.”

That’s $160K to $200K loaded, three to six months to find, and one generalist starting from zero. The retainer is less, starts on a date, and ends with notice. If you hire later, we’ll help them take it over.

“AI isn’t secure enough for our data.”

Everything runs in your Azure tenant under your policies. Your data never leaves and never trains anyone’s model. We put that in the contract.

“Can we start smaller?”

The assessment is smaller. It’s the start. It also tells you what not to build, which is usually worth more than the first tool.

“Can you do it hourly?”

We can, at $175 an hour, without the roadmap or the response times. Most companies find the flat number easier to budget and easier to defend.

“We have Copilot. Aren’t we covered?”

Copilot helps one person write one thing faster. It doesn’t know your project managers re-key the same data into three systems. The expensive problems in your company are specific; that’s what we build for.
